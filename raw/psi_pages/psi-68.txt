Objectives
The primary goal of the FLEX-2 flight experiments is to extend the results of FLEX-1 to fuels and
environmental conditions that mimic real combustor conditions, as well as, provide benchmark experimental
data on bi-component droplet combustion over a wide range of initial conditions in order to validate numerical
simulations and to develop new theoretical models.
Detailed experimental objectives can be further itemized as:
1. Investigate the role of liquid-phase mixing on the burning characteristics of bi-component fuel droplets.
2. Examine the steady and unsteady liquid and gas-phase phenomena in pure fuel combustion.
3. Study flame extinction mechanisms and their boundaries.
4. Analyze the soot formation process, including soot volume fraction soot-shell dynamics.
5. Determine the influence of droplet interactions on the flammability limits and extinction behavior of droplet
arrays.
6. Validate theoretical models of droplet combustion through comprehensive experimental data collection in
microgravity environments.
Approach
The chamber was filled with the appropriate atmosphere, which was varied in pressure between 0.5 atm to
3.0 atm and O2 concentration between 0.1 to 0.4 mole fraction with the diluent gas making up the balance. A
settling time of approximately 2 minutes elapsed prior to initiating the test in order to ensure that the
temperature and pressure of the chamber gases have stabilized. This settling time was followed by the
dispensing of a predetermined amount of fuel (based on the target droplet size) onto the support fiber for
those tests in which the droplet is not freely deployed. When sufficient fuel has been dispensed the
dispensing needles were retracted and a dwell period of at least 10 seconds was allowed for the droplet
internal fluid motion induced by deployment to subside. This was then followed by initiating power to the
ignitor for a selectable amount of time ranging from 1 second to 5 seconds after which the ignitor was
retracted from the field of view. If the flow field is to be generated by translating the droplet then droplet
motion would commence at the same time that the ignitor is retracted. A near real-time download of the color
camera video was employed in order to verify successful droplet deployment, ignition, and overall progress

The overall investigation approach can be summarized as:
1. Test parameters are determined, including pressure, temperature, fuel mixture, and humidity.
2. Fill the combustion chamber with the selected gas mixtures.
3. Deploy the droplet(s) carefully controlling the droplet diameter.
4. Allow the droplet to stabilize in the microgravity environment.
5. Ignite the droplet in a uniform and controlled fashion.
6. Monitor combustions and track flame evolution using high speed photography.
7. Monitor and adjust experimental conditions as needed.
8. Analyze data.
The independently varied experimental parameters during FLEX-2 experiments were 1) Fuel type 2) Ambient
gas composition 3) Ambient Pressure 4) Droplet initial diameter 5) Droplet translation velocity (and
acceleration) 6) Droplet spacing and initial size in a binary linear array.
Hypothesis
Fairly sophisticated numerical simulations of convective droplet combustion have been developed in the last
decade where the key contribution is the inclusion of full Navier-Stokes equations for the gas-phase flow
field. However, due to the inherent complexity of the problem, varying degrees of approximations have been
made in treating the chemistry, radiation, and the coupling between the gas and liquid phases. The validity of
the assumptions or the hypothesis used in these simulation have not been confirmed by using benchmark
data. By conducting these experiments in a microgravity environment, the effects of buoyancy are minimized,
and the droplet can be characterized only as a function of thermocapillary or solutalcapillary stresses
allowing for a clear observation of the fundamental combustion processes, The present FLEX-2 experiments
using bi-component mixtures. In addition, the microgravity environment allows study of such phenomena as
the transitions between radiative and diffusive extension of the flame. The primary goal of the flight
experiments is to provide benchmark experimental data on bi-component droplet combustion over a wide
range of initial conditions that could be used to validate numerical simulations and to develop new theoretical
models.
Research Impacts/Earth Benefits
The combustion of liquid fuels is the overwhelming energy source in the transportation sector. Insights
gained from these studies can lead to more efficient and cleaner combustion technologies which are crucial
for reducing pollution and greenhouse gas emissions. By improving fuel efficiency and developing better
fuels, these experiments can contribute to:
1) More sustainable energy production.
2) Future combustor designs with minimal carbon footprint, maximum fuel efficiency and less pollutant
emissions.
3) Allow quantitative evaluation of the performance of future fuels in combustors through the development of
surrogates (mixtures of pure fuels that simulate the behavior of real fuels).
4) Validation of jet engine models by engine manufacturers with support from prior droplet results.
