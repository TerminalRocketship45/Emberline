Objectives
The focus of DAFT / DAFT-2 was to mitigate the risk for the Smoke Aerosol Measurement Experiment
(SAME). SAME relied on a commercially available condensate nuclei counter, the TSI P-Trak, to quantify
smoke aerosols in a microgravity environment, however there were concerns that th P-Trak would not be
reliable in microgravity as it relies on gravity to recycle isopropyl alcohol used in the measurement process.
DAFT / DAFT-2 tests that a modified P-Trak particulate counter would be accurate in microgravity.
Summarized objectives include:
1) Demonstrate the Functionality of the Modified TSI P-Trak in Microgravity
2) Compare the TSI P-Trak Performance against the DustTrak control
3) Create and Analyze Aerosol Standards in Microgravity
4) Ensure the reliability of future aerosol experiments
Approach
The experimental approach consists of the following components:
1) Modification of Instrumentation: TSI P-Trak was modified for use in microgravity by redesigning the
condenser tube to use capillary forces for recycling isopropyl alcohol and removing the mercury switch
responsible for detecting instrument orientation.
2) Aerosol Generation: Arizona Red Dust (ARD) was chosen as an aerosol standard due to its ease of
dispersion. The ARD was aerosolized with GN2 to create particle samples. The aerosol was contained within
15-liter Mylar bags.
3) On-Orbit Testing: The modified P-Trak gathered data from counting aerosol particles and these data were
compared to the DustTrak CNC specifically designed to work in microgravity.
Hypothesis
The TSI P-Trak operates by condensing isopropanol around aerosol particulates that are subsequently laser
counted. It was hypothesized that the P-Trak would not work in microgravity due to two potential failure
modes that exist. 1) the system would clog or become obscured by isopropanol, and 2) the internal air-flow
dynamics of the optics chamber of the device will cause an over counting of particulates.

The TSI P-Trak was modified for microgravity use via using capillary action to recycle the isopropanol.
Therefore, there are two basic hypotheses:
1) that the P-Trak, as modified for use in SAME, would function normally in microgravity.
2) that the aerosol created and filtered per the experiment design would be within the measurement
capabilities of the test devices.
Research Impacts/Earth Benefits
Extended duration space flight missions with larger vessels will require fire detection systems with increased
reliability and sensitivity. This research will impact the development of novel fire and smoke detection
systems for the International Space Station and future spacecrafts. Additionally, the successful adaptation of
the TSI P-Trak for microgravity can inform the design of instruments for use in other challenging Earth
environments, such as submarines or underwater laboratories where accurate detection of smoke can save
lives.
