Objectives
The objectives of this investigation are to:
1) Examine the combustion behavior of n-dodecane and mixtures of n-dodecane/iso-dodecane at pressures
ranging from 0.25 atm to 5 atm.
2) Analyze the effects of helium dilution on cool flame behavior.
3) Examine the behavior of combustion characteristics.
4) Investigate the relationship between droplet size, pressure, and flame extinction, and (5) understand the
consequence of soot formation during cool flame combustion.
Approach
The Combustion Integrated Rack (CIR) contains the Multi-User Droplet Combustion Apparatus (MDCA)
which facilitates the controlled combustion of fuel droplets. A system of gas bottles and fuel syringes delivers
the desired gas mixture to the combustion chamber ranging from 0.25 to 0.5 atm. Droplets of fuel are
dispensed and suspended between hypodermic needles or tethered using silicon-carbide fibers. A hot-wire
igniter is positioned near the droplet for ignition. After ignition the wire is retracted. Fuels observed include n-
dodecane, farnesane, and mixtures of n-dodecane/iso-dodecane. Fuels diluted with molecular oxygen,
atomic nitrogen, and helium to study the influence on flame extinction and dynamics. Flames were observed
with a black-and-white camera for droplet size measurements. A UV camera was used for capturing the OH*
radical emissions and flame size. Other high resolution cameras were used for monitoring and
documentation. Narrow-band and wide-band radiometers for analyzed flame radiation.
Hypothesis
Flame combustion of liquid hydrocarbon fuels such as n-dodecane, n-dodecane/iso-dodecane mixtures, and
faresane in microgravity can provide insights into low-temperature chemical kinetics, revealing critical
parameters like droplet extinction diameters and flame dynamics that are not observable on Earth.
Research Impacts/Earth Benefits
A basic understanding of cool flames is important for understanding ignition phenomena of practical fuels and
fuel additives. Cool flames play a key role in the development and selection of new fuels and the design of
advanced engines such as clean, high-efficiency Homogeneous Charge Compression Ignition (HCCI)
